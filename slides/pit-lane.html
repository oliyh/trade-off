  • Small Org was small and focussed
  • It was successful, and began to grow

Symptoms of scale

  • Byzantine processes
  • Missing or conflicting requirements
  • One size fits all
  • Wrong incentives
  • Uncodified wisdom
  • Weird symptoms appear
  • (Read the list)
  • Old systems and processes are really hard to kill
  • It's a function of size and complexity that outscales individuals by multiple orders of magnitude
  • It is tough
“If these things are going to drive you insane,
Big Org might not be for you.”
  • This is a serious point, because it's really not for everyone
  • I have seen many times people leave after 6 months, which is in no-one's interests - why I'm being honest
  • But it's a huge opportunity to make your mark at scale and be part of something big, so let's talk about how to achieve that

04

Added value

  • With your fresh perspective and experience you can look around and identify other things that can be improved
  • There's this Local Maximum Paradox which says you've reached a peak, but not the highest peak you can reach, but you have to go down to go up
  • Sometimes you can be blind to the fact that there's a fundamentally better approach, because you've honed your current one for so long
  • There's also sunk costs, and never-ending urgent business deliveries that won't let you slow down
04

Big differences

  • Business benefits
  • Secondary benefits
  • Making these big differences means getting the business on your side - or at least not objecting
  • You need to speak their language - they don't care if you use git or containers or lambdas or distributed queues or immutable time series databases
  • They care about short lead times, frequent releases, fewer bugs
  • Secondary benefits like hiring and transferable skills
“Feature X requested on Wednesday,
in production on Friday.”
  • Measure yourself by what they care about
  • Champion your successes - I made a monthly newsletter and the business loved reading stats like this

Visible delivery

  • Regular demos
  • Continuous iteration
  • Especially when the team is new, you need to earn trust
  • Demonstrate you've understood the problem - regular and frequent
  • Show them you are working with them, using their feedback

Staying sustainable

  • Tax technical debt
  • Clear responsibilities
  • Clean interfaces
  • If the journey is long we need to make sure we can keep going at an even speed
  • Continually eliminate accidental drag
  • Do your best to avoid inherent drag
  • These things are great for observability, tests, automation
  • Releases at Big Org are hard work - convince people, paperwork burden
  • Very manual, and landed on me
  • Automated it by any means possible
  • Solution is now codified, scalable, less painful
